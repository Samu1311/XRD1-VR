**Reflections**

The VR project represented a more technically demanding and unpredictable phase of the XRD course. While it built upon the Unity skills gained during the AR project, it introduced additional complexity due to larger environments, interactive elements, and a heavier reliance on collaborative workflows. Although the project ultimately succeeded, the development process proved significantly more challenging.

At the start of the VR project, the group quickly agreed on a clear concept and divided responsibilities. The application consisted of four themed rooms, with each team member taking responsibility for designing and implementing one room. This structure made task allocation straightforward and allowed each member a high degree of creative freedom.

Development required extensive time in the XR lab, where even minor adjustments demanded careful testing. I found this phase particularly rewarding, as VR environments can be experienced almost immediately after changes are made. Seeing the results of my work in a fully immersive space was both motivating and satisfying. I was proud not only of my own room but also of the environments created by my teammates, especially due to the inclusion of engaging puzzle elements that enhanced user interaction.

However, major issues emerged during the GitHub merging phase. The project contained large files, including animations and prefabs, which GitHub struggled to handle efficiently. Commits often had to be pushed in batches, sometimes taking hours to complete. Merge conflicts became the most significant obstacle, consuming more time than active development.

After lengthy merge processes and extended Unity loading times, assets such as textures, prefabs, or scripts would occasionally disappear. Even more confusingly, repeating or aborting merges caused different elements to vanish or reappear, making the issues difficult to trace and resolve. This created a highly frustrating development environment and significantly impacted group morale.

On the day of the presentation, the project appeared to function correctly during morning checks. However, shortly before presenting, the teleportation system suddenly stopped working. Although we managed to fix the teleportation issue in time, one of the rooms remained non-functional due to time constraints, making the presentation particularly stressful.

Despite the challenges, the VR project was ultimately delivered as a functional application. However, the persistent GitHub and merge-related issues reduced the overall enjoyment of the development process. That said, the experience was extremely educational. It highlighted the importance of workflow planning, version control strategies, and asset management in complex XR projects. In retrospect, the difficulties encountered were as valuable as the development itself, providing insights that will strongly inform how I approach large-scale collaborative projects in the future.
