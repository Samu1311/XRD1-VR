# 🧭 Blog Post 01 – Choosing the Idea

## 📖 Project Context
This semester’s XR Development course at **VIA University College** challenges us to design and build an interactive **Virtual Reality (VR)** experience using **Unity**.  
Our goal is to not only showcase our technical understanding of XR systems, but also to design an experience that is **immersive, educational, and fun**.

After several brainstorming sessions exploring various ideas — from futuristic simulations to training systems — our team decided to take a creative route that combines **historical curiosity** with **puzzle-solving gameplay**.

---

## 🕰️ Concept: *Forward to the Past*

**Forward to the Past** is a **VR puzzle adventure** that takes players on a journey through **four ancient civilizations**:  

1. 🦜 **Ancient American Civilizations** (Inca, Maya, Aztec)  
2. 🐉 **Ancient China**  
3. 🏺 **Ancient Egypt**  
4. 🏛️ **Ancient Greece**  

In each “**time room**,” players will step into a distinct historical environment inspired by one of these cultures.  
The goal in each room is to **solve 2–3 puzzles** that reflect elements of the respective civilization — from engineering and architecture to language, symbols, and mythology.  
Once all puzzles in a room are completed, a **portal** activates, allowing the player to travel to the next era.

---

## 🧩 Gameplay Vision

The player assumes the role of a **time traveler** experimenting with a device capable of moving through time.  
Each room represents a different epoch of human civilization, where the player must use **logic, observation, and interaction** to overcome challenges rooted in that era’s culture and technology.

While the rooms are currently designed as **standalone chambers**, they will be connected through the player’s continuous progression — creating a rhythm of discovery, learning, and reward as time unfolds in reverse order.

### Puzzle Design Principles
- Each puzzle should feel **authentic to its era** — using cultural elements as part of its logic.  
- The gameplay will combine **cognitive puzzles** (symbols, patterns, language) and **mechanical puzzles** (locks, levers, alignments).  
- Interaction should rely on **intuitive VR mechanics** such as grabbing, rotating, combining, or placing objects.  
- No single “universal mechanic” — each room will introduce something new to maintain freshness.

---

## 🎨 Artistic Direction

The visual approach will follow a **semi-realistic art style**, balancing immersion with performance on VR headsets.  
Each environment will feature:
- Unique **lighting**, **textures**, and **soundscapes** to evoke the feeling of the specific time period.  
- Thematic **props and architecture**, referencing historical symbols and materials.  
- Subtle **ambient audio cues** — nature sounds, cultural music, or environmental effects — to enhance immersion.

---

## 🎯 Project Goals

Beyond building a playable experience, our objectives are to:

- Demonstrate **Unity’s VR capabilities** and interaction systems.  
- Explore **educational potential** within immersive environments.  
- Design and implement **era-specific puzzles** that encourage curiosity and engagement.  
- Produce a **fully functional VR prototype** within 5 weeks, ready for testing and presentation.

---

## 🧠 Why We Chose This Idea

We wanted a project that would let us **showcase technical skills** (interaction, triggers, 3D environments, sound) while also delivering a **memorable and meaningful player experience**.  

“Forward to the Past” gives us:
- Creative freedom to mix art, logic, and storytelling.  
- A clear structure of **four distinct rooms**, ideal for parallel team work.  
- A theme that is inherently **educational** and accessible to all audiences.  
- Opportunities to test **different VR mechanics** across environments.

---

## 🗓️ Next Steps

- Week 1 will focus on **setting up Unity XR**, **defining base interactions**, and **blocking out the first time room (Ancient American Civilizations)**.  
- Parallel work will start on **puzzle design sketches** and **asset references** for each civilization.  
- We’ll continue documenting every step of the process — from technical milestones to design reflections — in this blog series.
