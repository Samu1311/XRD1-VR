# Blog Post 04 – Prototyping Puzzles

## 🧩 Puzzle Experiments Begin
This was the most experimental week so far. Everyone began prototyping puzzle ideas for their rooms.

In the case of Mesoamerica for examplel, we initially wanted a **rotating Mayan calendar puzzle** made of multiple rings. However:
- the model didn't have separate rings  
- splitting them manually was time-consuming  
- interaction became unpredictable  
- snapping logic felt inconsistent  

After several attempts, it became clear that this puzzle would not be stable or intuitive enough.

## 🔍 Exploring Alternatives
Instead of forcing a broken concept, we shifted to simpler mechanics:
- pressure plates  
- object placement  
- proximity triggers  
- glowing feedback  

Samuele prototyped these using primitive shapes and placeholder scripts — no polish yet, just core logic.

## 🚶 Team Prototypes
The rest of the team developed:
- **Egypt** → collect items to satisfy the Sphinx  
- **China** → hit a temple gong to unlock the door  
- **Greece** → an oracle interaction + mythical vase displays  

## 🧠 Reflection
This week taught me an important lesson:  
**the smartest puzzle is not the most complex — it’s the one the player understands immediately.**

The pressure plate concept felt much more VR-friendly.

## 🔭 Next Steps
Turn prototypes into **proper prefabs** and integrate them visually into each room.
