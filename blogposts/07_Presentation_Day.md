# Blog Post 07 – Presentation Day at the XR Lab

## 🎤 Showcasing the Project
The final week concluded with a live presentation at the XR Lab. The teacher tested all four rooms in real VR equipment.

At first, we had a moment of **utter panic**: the build we pushed didn’t load the final changes.

Fortunately, we quickly fixed the import mistake and deployed a fresh working build.

## 🧪 How the Test Went
Most of the flow worked:
- Start in Main  
- Enter each civilization room  
- Solve the puzzles  
- Move to the next era  
- Experience unique environments

There was one of the rooms where the behaviour got broken because of changes in another room, so our last link from Greece to Egypt did not work... sad. Additionally, Egypt had some issues with the usual puzzle behaviour, so - once again, updates and merging were resulting in some weird and anxiety-inducing moments. However, we were determined to fix these issues before submitting the final repository and video! 

The experience itself taught us also that people would appreciate more direction in the puzzles they have to do, and it was also just interesting to see how people understood interactions with the puzzles — especially the pressure plates and the Chinese gong - and in general enjoyed the large environments we created. At least some successes, yay!

## 👥 Team Synergy
Everyone's room felt distinct but cohesive. Despite the merge struggles, the final result delivered exactly what we wanted:  
**four small but meaningful historical VR experiences.**

## 🧠 Final Reflections
This project taught us:
- how to build and structure XR projects  
- how fragile camera rigs and prefabs can be  
- the importance of early planning  
- how valuable teamwork becomes under pressure  

## 🏁 Conclusion
Seven weeks ago we were brainstorming ideas.  
Today we have a functional VR time-travel experience.

Forward to the Past is complete, and that is gratifying enough on its own.
